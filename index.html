Abstract

How can a robot turn an instruction such as “place the eraser on the box” into a sequence of physical interactions? In Objectives as Policies (OaP), a pretrained vision-language model writes an objective program for a shared sampling-based model-predictive controller. Each stage specifies running costs, terminal costs, and conditions for advancing to the next stage.

We reconstruct the scene from RGB-D observations and run the program in this digital twin. The model can revise the program using feedback from simulated executions. On the physical robot, the controller follows the fixed program and replans motions from the measured state. We evaluate this approach on four tabletop tasks in simulation and on hardware.

Method

In one simulated pick-and-place trial, the robot was still holding the eraser at the end of the run. The release stage asked the gripper to open while rewarding it for holding the eraser. After reviewing the rollout, the model removed that cost and split release and settling into separate stages. On the next execution, the robot placed the eraser on the box and let go.

Results

With the cost library and self-review, OaP succeeded in 26 of 40 simulation trials (65%) before execution feedback. With up to two feedback rounds, cumulative success reached 36 of 40 (90%), with at most three executions per trial.

On hardware, we tested 20 programs that had succeeded in simulation, each in two perturbed scenes. The robot completed the task in 32 of 40 executions (80%).
